# On Machines

Every book is a machine for reading, and every reading machine is, in the end, a
book that has learned to turn its own pages.

The sacred computer imagines the two as one: parchment and terminal, ink and
phosphor — the warmth of old paper under the cool logic of a screen. A tome is
rendered, not printed, yet it should *feel* printed, because the reader's hands
remember paper even when the light comes from behind the glass.

This companion tome is proof of the smallest claim the library makes: that a
bibliotheca is simply more than one tome, catalogued and within reach.
